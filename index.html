Keep Original / Write Plain / Mark Unknown

Three Steps Across the Bridge

When a sheet or target page shows a label you do not fully understand, do not guess. Keep the original, write a plain note, and mark what is still unknown.

Step 01 — Keep Original

Preserve what the page shows

Copy the visible label exactly as it appears, and note where it came from. Do not silently replace an unfamiliar term with a familiar one.

Step 02 — Write Plain

Add a cautious working note

Write a short, restrained note in clear English about what the label appears to mean. A note helps you read — it does not prove anything.

Step 03 — Mark Unknown

Flag what lacks context

Put every ambiguous or context-dependent part in its own marked space, so it stays visibly open instead of silently assumed.

A Working Note Is Not a Product Fact

A Note Helps You Read, It Does Not Prove

A plain-language note is a reading aid, not a certificate. It cannot confirm an option, material, size, price, stock, seller, or platform status.

✓A working note is

  • A cautious restatement of a visible label.
  • Marked clearly where it is uncertain.
  • Re-checked against the actual page and option.

✕A working note is not

  • Proof of a material, size, price, or stock.
  • A translation or official description.
  • A verdict on the seller or the platform.
Parallel ruleOriginal wording and your note sit side by side and never merge. The note explains how you read it; the original remains the evidence.
Option Names Need Their Page

Read Options Against the Current Page

Size, color, quantity, and version are names that only make sense next to the page you actually opened and the option you actually selected.

Size A size label is relative to the item

The same size label can mean different measurements across products. Keep the original label, and re-check the actual dimensions or fit on the current page instead of assuming the name is absolute.

Color A color name needs its swatch

A color name is only meaningful beside the option you selected. Two listings can share a name and show different shades, so hold the name against the current page.

Quantity A quantity name needs its count

Single, set, and multi-piece are distinct options. Re-check what the selected quantity actually includes on the current page before relying on the name.

Version A version name needs its context

A version label depends on the generation, material, or spec it refers to. Mark it as open until you can tie the name to the page and the selection you made.

Where the Bridge Stops

Know When to Stop Reading

  • 1

    The label is not preserved. You replaced an unfamiliar term with a familiar one. Stop and restore the original wording.

  • 2

    The note is drifting into claims. Your note now asserts a material, price, or stock. Stop and dial it back to what the page shows.

  • 3

    Context is missing. The meaning depends on a page or option you have not opened. Stop and mark it unknown instead of guessing.

  • 4

    Translations conflict. Two readings disagree. Stop and re-check the source rather than picking the one you prefer.

  • 5

    The note proves nothing. It has become a certificate instead of a reading aid. Stop and separate the note from the fact.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a CNShopper spreadsheet?

A CNShopper spreadsheet is a shared document that compiles listing references, category groupings, and product links formatted for use with agent shopping services. It is a reference to research, not an official, live, or complete product catalog.

Is this website the official CNShopper service?

No. This website is an independent informational and navigation resource. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated by CNShopper or Google.

What is a Plain-Language Bridge?

A Plain-Language Bridge is a way to read a label you do not fully understand. You keep the original visible wording, write a cautious plain-language note, mark anything ambiguous or missing context, and re-check against the actual page and option.

Does this site translate listings or verify products?

No. This resource does not translate, verify, or certify anything. A plain-language note only helps you read; it does not prove an option, material, size, price, stock, seller, or platform status.

How do I write a plain-language note?

Preserve the original visible label first, then write a short, cautious note in clear English about what it appears to mean, and mark any part that is ambiguous or missing context. Always re-check against the actual target page and the specific option you selected.

Where do I continue after reading a label?

After you have preserved the label, written your note, and re-checked the actual page, you can continue through an agent shopping service to submit the item. This website is a navigation resource and does not process orders itself.
